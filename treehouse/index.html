Why Treehouse

Small enough to know every child by name

With twelve classes and 190 students, our teachers know each child’s reading level, favorite book and best friend. That attention is how children here grow confident, and how problems are caught early.

Small classes

No more than 16 children in a class, with a teacher and an assistant in Kindergarten and Grade 1.

Specialist teachers

Music, art, PE, Spanish and STEM are taught by specialists from Kindergarten, in rooms built for each subject.

Learning outdoors

Every class spends one afternoon a week in our forest classroom, whatever the weather.

Care that fits work

Before care from 7:30 am and after care until 6 pm, with homework help, clubs and a hot snack.

Learning by grade

What your child learns, year by year

Each grade builds on the last. Pick a grade to see the big goals, a signature project and what a week looks like.

Kindergarten Ages 5–6

Kindergarten is about falling in love with school. Children learn through play, stories and hands-on projects while building the letter sounds, number sense and friendships that everything else rests on.

  • Daily phonics in small groups of four to six
  • Counting, sorting and shapes with hands-on materials
  • Choice time in the block, art and reading corners
  • A weekly afternoon in the forest classroom

Signature project

The Kindergarten Café: children plan a menu, make price tags and run a pretend café for their families.

Grade 1 Ages 6–7

In Grade 1, reading clicks. Children move from sounding out words to reading short books on their own, write their first stories and learn addition and subtraction through games and real problems.

  • Guided reading with books matched to each child
  • Writer’s workshop: stories with a beginning, middle and end
  • Addition and subtraction within 20
  • Science units on light, sound and living things

Signature project

The Class Seed Bank: children grow beans in light, dark, wet and dry pots, then report what they found.

Grade 2 Ages 7–8

Grade 2 builds fluency and independence. Readers move into chapter books, writers learn to organize paragraphs, and young mathematicians work with numbers to 1,000, money and time.

  • Chapter books and a first book club
  • Paragraph writing with topic sentences
  • Place value to 1,000, money and telling time
  • A community study of how Maple Hollow works

Signature project

The Maple Hollow Guidebook: each child writes and illustrates a page about a favorite place in town.

Grade 3 Ages 8–9

Grade 3 is the jump from learning to read to reading to learn. Children research topics, write reports, master multiplication facts and start setting their own learning goals.

  • Novel studies and short research reports
  • Multiplication and division facts to 10 × 10
  • Fractions, area and measurement
  • Life cycles and forces in the science lab

Signature project

The Butterfly Project: the class raises painted lady caterpillars, keeps a science journal and releases the butterflies in the garden.

Grade 4 Ages 9–10

In Grade 4 children write to persuade, argue with evidence and tackle multi-step math. They take on more responsibility too, from class jobs to buddy reading with Kindergarten.

  • Persuasive writing, poetry and literature circles
  • Multi-digit multiplication and equivalent fractions
  • Electric circuits and the Earth’s changing surface
  • Buddy reading with a Kindergarten partner

Signature project

The Invention Fair: pairs design a gadget that solves a real school problem and pitch it to a panel of parents.

Grade 5 Ages 10–11

Our oldest students lead the school. Grade 5 prepares children for middle school with longer projects, study skills, public speaking and a three-day outdoor trip in the spring.

  • Argument essays and novel seminars
  • Decimals, fraction operations and volume
  • Ecosystems, space and chemical changes
  • Student council and assembly leadership

Signature project

The Grade 5 Exhibition: a six-week research project on a question each child chooses, presented to the whole school.
